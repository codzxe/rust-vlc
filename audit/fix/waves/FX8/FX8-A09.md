## The four latent defect classes I found in the inherited runner

Each was caught by the runner's own assertions/guards — the broken draft
exited 1 ("8 green control(s) went red"), never a silent green:

1. **Single-statement `local` cross-reference** (mk_copy line): `local
   name="$1" dst="$WORK/tree-$name"` aborts under `set -u` — all words of a
   `local` expand BEFORE the builtin assigns (probed standalone:
   "name: unbound variable"). mk_copy returned an empty path; 11 red rows
   "asserted" at rc=127 with no gate ever run. Closed twice: split locals,
   AND `red()` now rejects rc 126/127 as "gate not runnable" (a harness
   failure is never a red proof).
2. **die() inside command substitution**: mk_copy's die only exits the
   subshell `c="$(mk_copy pN)"` runs in — the abort was swallowed and the
   run continued on an empty path. Closed by `|| die` chained at all 17
   mk_copy call sites (documented at the function).
3. **Unanchored tar exclusion**: `--exclude=audit` (intent: the 448 MB root
   audit/ evidence tree) also stripped `docs/audit/` from every copy — GNU
   tar matches unanchored patterns against any trailing component
   sequence (probed with a toy tree). The copy-completeness check caught
   it; closed with `./`-anchored exclusions.
4. **Checkers grepped comments**: the real rust-clippy.yml's own fix-notes
   ("continue-on-error: true removed") made G7 fail on a correct file.
   Closed by `yaml_body()` comment-stripping in all three structural
   checkers (verified: green on the real files, red on the broken fixture).

(A 4b of my own making, caught by `bash -n` before any run: while writing
the anti-double-quoted-backtick prose I authored a double-quoted backtick —
and an unescaped `$a` in double quotes that `bash -n` does NOT catch was
caught by the next run. Both fixed; `bash -n` is now part of the regen
flow.)
